Doctrine of the Sin Unto Death

  1. Definition.
    1. The sin unto death is the means by which the reversionistic believer transfers from time to eternity (1 John 5:16; Ps. 118:17–18). He is removed from phase two under the administration of maximum punitive punishment without benefit of dying grace.
    2. The sin unto death involves a painful, horrible, miserable death as well as loss of blessing, both in time and eternity.
    3. No believer will suffer or be miserable in eternity (Rev. 21:4); hence, the sin unto death is God's greatest and last expression of divine punishment to the reversionistic believer.
  2. Reversionism is the cause of the sin unto death (Jer. 9:13–16; 44:12; Phil. 3:18–19; Rev. 3:15–16).
  3. The administration of the sin unto death does not mean loss of salvation (2 Tim. 2:11–13).
  4. Case histories of the sin unto death.
    1. For monetary reversionism: greed, dishonesty, and approbation lust of Ananias and Sapphira (Acts 5:1–10).
    2. For phallic reversionism: the incest of a Corinthian believer (1 Cor. 5:1–5).
    3. For ritual reversionism: the participation in Communion by carnal or reversionistic believers (1 Cor. 11:30–31).
    4. For mental attitude reversionism: Saul's refusal to fight, to kill King Agag, and to obey the Lord (1 Sam. 13:9–14; 15; 1 Chron. 10:13–14).
    5. For verbal reversionism: maligning and trouble making by Hymenaeus and Alexander (1 Tim. 1:19–20).
    6. For antiestablishment reversionism: the human viewpoint foreign policy of King Hezekiah (Isa. 30:1–3; 31:1–3; 38).
  5. Reversion recovery cancels the sin unto death.
    1. Rebound (1 Cor. 11:30–31).
    2. Repentance (change of mental attitude toward doctrine) (2 Tim. 2:24–26).
    3. The function of GAP (James 4:4–8).
